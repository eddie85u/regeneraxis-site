# RegenerAxis Web Copy Compliance Guide

Purpose: keep all public-facing copy safe under Colombian health
advertising rules (INVIMA context) and safe for a US/international
audience (FTC/FDA-adjacent expectations). This is a working guide,
not legal advice. Final copy should be reviewed by a Colombian
health-regulatory lawyer before major campaigns.

## Core principles
1. Sell programs and outcomes, never molecules. No compound, peptide,
   or drug names anywhere on the site.
2. Educational tone, not promotional claims about curing or treating
   disease.
3. Every treatment claim is conditional and process-based: what the
   program is designed to support, not what it guarantees.
4. Medicine first: every page reinforces that programs start with a
   medical assessment and are physician supervised.

## DO
- Use: "designed to support", "may help", "aims to", "supports",
  "personalized to your biology", "under medical supervision".
- Say results vary by individual, on every program page.
- Frame benefits as wellness and performance outcomes: energy,
  recovery, sleep quality, body composition, vitality.
- Include the anti-doping advisory on athletic programs.
- Keep the educational disclaimer on the Science section and the
  global disclaimer in the footer.
- Describe the process (assessment, labs, protocol, follow-up) in
  detail. Process claims are safe claims.
- FAQ answers follow the same claims rules: process-based, no promised
  timeframes or outcomes, always pointing back to the medical assessment.

## DON'T
- Never claim to treat, cure, prevent, or reverse any disease or
  medical condition (diabetes, arthritis, depression, cancer, etc.).
- Never name specific compounds, peptides, hormones, or drug brand
  names in public copy.
- Never promise specific results, numbers, or timeframes
  ("lose 10kg in 3 months", "look 10 years younger").
- Never use "guaranteed", "proven", "miracle", "no side effects",
  "100% safe", "clinically proven" (unless citing a specific study
  approved by the medical director).
- Never imply treatments replace conventional medical care.
- Never use before/after imagery implying guaranteed outcomes.
- Never publish testimonials that make disease claims or specific
  quantified results; edit quotes to experience-based language.
- No em dashes anywhere in site copy (brand style rule).

## Testimonial rules
- Quotes describe experience and service, not medical outcomes.
  OK: "I felt stronger and slept better throughout my program."
  NOT OK: "It cured my chronic tendinitis."
- Identify by first name + country. Get written consent on file.
- Add a line near testimonial sections: "Individual experiences.
  Results vary."

## Pricing
- Prices may be shown as "Investment from..." with a note that final
  pricing depends on medical assessment and personalization.
